Concrete Grinding in Rochester Hills, MI

Epoxy and other coatings fail early when they're applied over a sealed or contaminated concrete surface. Grinding mechanically opens the pores of the concrete so whatever goes on top actually bonds and lasts, instead of peeling within a year or two.

What's Included

  • Mechanical grinding of the full concrete surface
  • Removal of existing sealers, coatings, or contamination that would block adhesion
  • Profiling the concrete to the right texture for the coating going on top
  • Crack and joint assessment before coating
  • A clean, dust-cleared surface ready for coating

How We Do It

1

Inspect the Slab

We check the existing surface for old sealers, coatings, cracks, and overall condition.

2

Grind

The floor is mechanically ground to remove contamination and open the surface profile.

3

Address Cracks & Joints

Cracks and joints are noted and addressed as part of prep before coating.

4

Clean the Surface

Dust and debris are cleared so the floor is ready for the next step, whether that's epoxy or another coating.

When You Need This

Any concrete floor about to be epoxy coated needs grinding first, especially if it's previously been sealed or painted. It's also useful on its own when a floor needs old coatings or contamination removed before any new finish is decided on.

Common Questions

Is grinding always necessary before epoxy?

For a coating that actually bonds and lasts, yes. Skipping proper surface prep is one of the most common reasons epoxy floors fail early.

Does grinding create a lot of dust?

Grinding does generate dust. We take reasonable steps to manage it during the job.

Can grinding fix an uneven floor?

Grinding can smooth minor high spots and remove surface material, but it isn't a substitute for structural concrete repair on a severely uneven slab.

Do you grind floors that already have a failing coating?

Yes, removing an old, failing coating and re-profiling the concrete is a common reason to grind before recoating.
